Televisions

Energy Use of Televisions

Televisions are one of the most common home entertainment devices in Australian households. Due to their frequent use, they can contribute significantly to electricity consumption, especially in homes with multiple TVs.

Energy Standards and Ratings

In Australia, televisions must comply with Minimum Energy Performance Standards (MEPS). Most TVs also come with an Energy Rating Label, which helps consumers understand how energy-efficient a model is.

The Energy Rating Label allows users to:

  • Compare different TV models
  • Identify energy-efficient options
  • Estimate electricity usage

Choosing a TV with a higher energy rating can help reduce long-term electricity costs.

Factors Affecting TV Energy Consumption

Several factors influence how much energy a television uses:

  • Screen size – Larger screens generally consume more power
  • Display technology – LED TVs are typically more efficient than older models
  • Brightness settings – Higher brightness increases energy usage
  • Usage time – Longer viewing hours increase total consumption

Tips to Reduce Energy Usage

Consumers can reduce the energy consumption of their televisions by:

  • Choosing energy-efficient models with higher ratings
  • Lowering screen brightness when possible
  • Turning off the TV when not in use
  • Avoiding leaving the TV on standby mode

Conclusion

Televisions play a major role in household energy consumption, but their impact can be reduced through smarter choices and usage habits. By selecting efficient models and managing usage, consumers can save energy and reduce electricity costs.

Questions

1. What type of TV screen technologies are currently available in Australia and which are the most frequent?

LED televisions are the most common, followed by LCD, while OLED adoption remains relatively low.

2. What screen sizes are available and which are most frequent?

75-inch televisions are the most common, followed by 55-inch models. Smaller 24-inch screens are the least frequent in this dataset.

3. Which brands have the largest number of models?

SAMSUNG dominates the market with 1197 models, followed by KOGAN (769 models) and LG (679 models). These three brands account for the majority of television models available in Australia. Smaller brands have significantly fewer model options in comparison.

4. Which type of screen technology consumes the least amount of power?

LED technology consumes the least amount of power at only 10W average, making it the most energy-efficient option. LCD is slightly more power-hungry at 13.4W, while OLED and Plasma consume significantly more power at 52W and 89W respectively. Choosing LED televisions can help reduce energy consumption and lower electricity bills.

5. What is the relationship between screen size and power use?

There is a clear positive relationship between screen size and power consumption. Larger televisions (46-58 inches) consume significantly more power, typically 100-155W, while smaller screens (19-24 inches) use only 19-50W. This shows that consumers should consider both the size and technology when selecting energy-efficient televisions.

8. Do higher star ratings actually mean lower power consumption?

The chart shows that higher star ratings do not always mean lower power consumption. While low-rating models like 1-star and 9-star sets can be quite efficient, some lower- to mid-rated TVs (2-star, 4-star, 7-star) average higher power use than many higher-rated models. This suggests that star rating alone is not a perfect indicator of energy efficiency, and consumers should still compare average power use when choosing a television.
