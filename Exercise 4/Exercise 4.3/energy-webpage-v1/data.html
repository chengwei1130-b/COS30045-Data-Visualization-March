Storyboard

Issue
Many households use televisions and monitors of different screen sizes daily. Larger screens are becoming more common, which can lead to higher electricity consumption and increased energy costs.
Demonstrate Issue
For example, a 65-inch TV generally consumes more electricity than a 32-inch TV when used for the same amount of time and brightness level. Over time, households with larger screens will experience higher electricity usage and bills.
Idea and Focus
How screen size affects energy consumption in display devices and how choosing the right screen size can help reduce unnecessary electricity usage in households.
Analysis
Energy consumption increases as screen size increases because larger displays require more backlighting and power to maintain brightness. Other factors such as resolution, brightness settings, and usage duration also influence total energy usage, but screen size remains a key contributor.
Evidence
Studies and energy rating data show that smaller screens generally consume less power compared to larger screens under similar usage conditions.
Recommendation
Households should choose screen sizes that match their actual needs instead of going for oversized displays.
