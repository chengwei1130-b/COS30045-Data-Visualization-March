Exercise 5 – TV Energy Consumption Charts

This page demonstrates four different chart types using D3.js, exploring TV energy consumption data and Australian electricity spot prices.

1. Scatter Plot – Australian Electricity Spot Prices by State (1998–2024)

Each point represents a state's electricity spot price for a given year. This chart shows how prices have varied across states and over time.

Prices were relatively stable through the 2000s before rising sharply from 2016. The 2021–2022 period saw the highest prices on record across most states.

2. Line Chart – Mean Energy Consumption by Screen Size and Technology

Each line represents a screen technology (LCD, LCD (LED), OLED). The x-axis shows screen size in inches and the y-axis shows mean annual energy consumption.

Energy consumption increases consistently with screen size across all technologies. OLED TVs consume the most energy at larger screen sizes.

3. Bar Chart – Energy Consumption by Screen Technology (55-inch TVs Only)

By fixing the screen size at 55 inches, this bar chart isolates the effect of screen technology on energy consumption.

At 55 inches, LCD uses less energy than LED on average, while OLED remains the most energy-intensive technology.

4. Donut Chart – Mean Energy Consumption by Screen Technology (All Sizes)

This donut chart compares the mean annual energy consumption (kWh/year) for LCD, LED, and OLED televisions across all available screen sizes.

OLED televisions consume considerably more energy on average than LCD and LED models, making them the least efficient technology type in this dataset.
